Where the arms actually disagree

Absolute difference between arms, ×4. 12-vs-9 (right) is nearly black — a single floating blob at hip height. Both comparisons against c15 light up along every silhouette edge: the 2 dB lives in edge and surface fidelity, not in any global shift.

Figure 3 · prop masking

The double bass must be foreground

Text-prompt "person" masking sends the bass to the static background plate, where it freezes and smears into a curtain of streaks across the player (left). Unioning a second SAM3 concept — "double bass" — into the performer's foreground restores the instrument (middle) and is worth +1.63 dB in the person region. Standing rule: anything a performer carries or plays is foreground.

Dance · camera ladder (clean test)

The knee sits between 5 and 10 uniform cameras — 10 saturates

24-camera rig, uniform greedy max-min subsets of the 20 train cameras, identical recipe and holdouts. Density varies while angular coverage stays gap-free — the confound that limited the bar ladder is absent. Halving 20 to 10 cameras costs 0.05 dB; dropping to 5 costs 4.1 dB. Together with the bar and PKU results: beyond ~10 uniformly placed cameras, count buys nothing for arc-style serving — placement uniformity is the binding requirement, and the planned 12-camera shell holds with margin.

Dance · background

The venue, not the method: glass walls break the background recipe

The dance background failed its 30 dB gate at 15.75 dB — and a controlled re-run on verified-clean plates failed identically, ruling out the masking incident as the cause. Decomposition: black rectification borders cost every camera 2–5 dB, and the two holdouts facing the glass wall (outdoor scenery, reflections — view-dependent content a 24-single-frame static fit cannot reconcile) are broken outright and degrade with training. The same recipe passed at 32.97 dB in a small matte studio. Consequence for our own rig: a matte studio is load-bearing for the background gate, so the dance page ships person-only. The person fit itself is the program's best real-footage result at 30.4 dB.
